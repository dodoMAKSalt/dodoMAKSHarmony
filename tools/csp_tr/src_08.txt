Unity
Unity 6 port only, needs NavMeshExperiment: routes of the player's own units are found on Unity's navigation mesh (well under a millisecond, in one go) instead of the game's search over the voxel graph, and turned back into the voxels the walk needs. Enemies, and any route the mesh cannot give, keep the game's search.
Unity 6 port only: builds Unity's navigation mesh from the route graph next to it and keeps it up to date; NavMeshRoutes routes the player's units over it (DevTools: /api/navmesh).
Unity 6 port, multiplayer: material delivered to a blueprint the other player's game does not have (an ally's blueprint, which only its own faction is sent) is hidden there, as it is in the blueprint, instead of lying in a heap that cannot be picked up.
Unity 6 port, multiplayer: once the host has pressed start, a client's "not ready" (pressed as the loading screen came up) is kept ready, the lobby's "everyone not ready" waits, a player joining in that moment is turned away and one leaving no longer holds up the others; host and clients sat at 0 % loaded for good before.
Unity 6 port, multiplayer: the players' worlds (terrain and placed blocks) are compared every [Multiplayer] DesyncCheckSeconds. Where they differ twice in a row, the majority's world is taken (the host's when the vote is even) and the others are corrected; the chat says so. Each peer works out by itself what falls when a structure loses its support, so the worlds could drift apart and stay apart.
Unity 6 port, multiplayer: up to 8 players in a hosted game (the game: 4, and at 4 its lobby left the online list); [Multiplayer] MaxPlayers, the host's setting applies. The map still decides how many factions there are; extra players share them.
Unity 6 port: a storage's pallet animation (the second after its content changed) is worked out by Unity's animation pass on its worker threads instead of on the main thread. Off: stepped on the main thread as the game does.
Unity 6 port: multiplayer over the internet through Steam without opening a port. A hosted game is listed in the online list at once (the game waited for Castle Story's NAT server, which is gone), the list shows games from all regions, and joining one (from the list or a friend's invitation) connects over Steam's peer-to-peer networking through Valve's relays. LAN games and typed-in addresses connect directly.
Unity 6 port: text boxes in the game's menus (the lobby's player name, ...) take what was typed on Enter or when left; the port's menus had lost that link, so the name could not be changed. What a player is typing in the lobby's name box is kept when the player list changes (another player's name, faction or team), so several players can rename themselves at once.
Unity 6 port: the faction vision's scan runs as a Burst job of the player on worker threads instead of on the main thread (the results are the same). Off: on the main thread.
